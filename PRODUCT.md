# PRODUCT.md

## Register

**brand.** This repository is the promotional site for GoatHam. The app itself
lives at `~/code/ontheapp` and is the product register; here the design is the
product, and a visitor's impression is the thing being made.

## Product purpose

GoatHam is an Android, iOS and Linux client for the outdoor amateur radio
programmes: Parks on the Air, Summits on the Air, World Wide Flora & Fauna and
Towers on the Air. It serves both sides of a contact. Hunting activators from
home, and being the activator on a summit with a pile-up to work.

What distinguishes it from the spot viewers already on the stores:

- Four programmes in one feed, never branched on in the interface.
- A reference is treated as an area, not a pin. Where a boundary is published,
  the app answers whether the operator is inside it, outside it, or too near
  the edge to be certain.
- Activations are derived from the log rather than stored, so nothing can
  disagree with the contacts after an edit, a delete or an import.
- The log backs up and restores as ADIF, which is the format every programme's
  uploader already takes.

## Users

Licensed amateur radio operators who carry a radio outdoors. They are technical,
they already own three other tools that do part of this, and they are unmoved by
claims. They respond to specifics: a band plan handled correctly, a UTC day that
rolls over when it should, a park boundary that is actually a polygon.

Secondary: hunters who never leave the house and want to know who is on the air
right now, and where.

## Brand personality

Printed, weathered, deliberate. The physical object is a silkscreened national
park poster nailed to a trailhead kiosk, sun-faded on one side. Flat colour,
heavy ink, no gloss. The app icon is already drawn in that language, so the site
scales the icon up rather than inventing a second identity beside it.

Voice: plain, specific, unhurried. States what the app does and stops. No
exclamation, no growth-marketing cadence, no "effortless" or "seamless".

## Anti-references

- The dark terminal aesthetic. Amateur radio software defaults to CRT green on
  black or a grey Win32 grid, and every alternative client on the stores looks
  like a CRT. That is the first-order category reflex and it is banned here.
- Editorial-typographic. Display serif italic, small mono labels, ruled columns.
  The second-order reflex, and the wrong register for a poster.
- Outdoorsy REI green. The programmes are about places, not about gear.
- SaaS landing page grammar: hero metric, three identical feature cards, a
  gradient, a testimonial carousel.

## Strategic design principles

1. **The icon is the brand.** Cream, navy, a low orange sun, slate ridges. The
   palette is sampled from the artwork, not invented alongside it.
2. **Show the product.** Real screenshots of the running app, framed so the
   app's own green interface reads as the product rather than as brand colour.
3. **Specifics over adjectives.** "A park can be sixty kilometres long" beats
   "powerful mapping".
4. **Colour carries meaning only inside the app.** Band colours appear in the
   screenshots because they mean something there. They are never borrowed for
   site chrome.
